## The problem

On a quadrant tactile sensor, the raised silicone bump is a **passive mechanical signal
conditioner**: it maps an applied force vector to a pressure field that a Velostat film samples
at four quadrants. Shear shows up as the *difference* across the footprint. The published
baseline picked its bump by eye-judging von Mises stress under a single 1&nbsp;N load — no
shape family, no aspect-ratio sweep, and no link to the pressure the film actually reads.

## The study, in three stages

1. **Reproduce and sweep.** Rebuild the baseline bump in nonlinear contact FEA (hyperelastic
   silicone, bonded base) and sweep its height.
2. **Compare profiles.** Five top-face shapes — flat punch, cylinder, crown, tapered crown,
   dome — pressed by one shared rigid indenter, with contact area left as an output.
3. **Re-sweep the winner's height.**

The output variable is contact pressure mapped through the measured Velostat R(p) curve — not
stress, which was the baseline's mistake.

The **crown** captures 98% of the best available shear signal while deflecting only 68% as much
as the dome. But a shape ranking is only meaningful if it isn't just a stiffness ranking in
disguise — so the study has a built-in control:

<Figure src="p2/chart_profile_control.png" panel wide alt="Two bar charts: the shear channel varies about a third across the four profiles while the normal channel varies only a few percent." caption="The control that makes the result trustworthy: top-face profile moves the shear channel by about a third across arms, while the normal channel barely moves. The ranking is a profile effect, not a compliance artifact." />

Re-sweeping the crown's height then showed there's **no interior optimum** to find:

<Figure src="p2/chart_height_scaling.png" panel wide alt="Log-log plot: shear gain scales as h^0.63 while deflection scales as h^1.14, so gain per unit deflection falls monotonically with height." caption="Shear signal grows as ~h^0.63 while deflection grows as ~h^1.14, so signal-per-deflection falls monotonically. ## Fabricating it

The bump sheets are cast in silicone from 3D-printed molds — several profiles at once, so the
FEA ranking could be checked on the bench — then integrated onto the Velostat and flex-PCB
stack.

## Where it stands

- **2.4&nbsp;mm is the bottom of the tested height ladder** — the best of six, not proven
  sufficient. The ladder needs extending downward until the signal actually fails the spec.
- Bump volume changes with taper — a known confound on that axis. It's measured, not
  eliminated, using the flat normal channel as the control.
- **Slip prediction:** I ran 50 grip-and-slip trials with sensor-equipped robotic grippers,
  recording synchronized tactile and vision data. That dataset now supports slip-prediction
  development.
